Who to watch. Who to root for.

Nov. 21, 9:18 p.m. Pacific: Oregon is 8–3 (6–2 Big Ten) after beating Michigan State 45–7. Washington beat Indiana 27–24 in overtime, Michigan beat UCLA 18–16 in triple overtime, and Nebraska beat Ohio State 31–28; Iowa beat Illinois 34–17. Oregon hosts Washington on Nov. 28. The Ducks were No. 13 in the Nov. 17 CFP rankings, which predate this week’s games.

The Big Ten title race opened up: Oregon is tied with Indiana and Iowa at 6–2, behind UCLA at 7–1. CBS Sports projects Oregon into the title game when Oregon, UCLA and Indiana all win; other combinations involve tiebreaks and conflicting reporting. Other combinations produce complicated tiebreakers, and published analyses disagree on some outcomes. An Oregon win is not an officially verified clinch; a 9–3 record does not guarantee an at-large CFP berth.

Texas, Notre Dame, Miami and Tennessee all won Saturday, so the shared graphic’s specific “lose both” conditions for those teams can no longer happen. That does not eliminate Oregon from the playoff; BYU, Indiana, Wake Forest and Texas Tech remain on the graphic’s watch list, subject to its stated conditions.

2026–27 playoff picture

The field has 12 teams: the ACC, Big Ten, Big 12 and SEC champions; the highest-ranked team from the American, Conference USA, MAC, Mountain West, Pac-12 or Sun Belt; and seven at-large teams. Notre Dame receives an automatic bid if it finishes in the final Top 12, reducing at-large bids to six. Seeds 1–4 get byes; seeds 5–8 host first-round games. Rankings are not the same as seeds.

Illustrative bracket from the Nov. 17 rankings, not official or live: assumes the highest-ranked team in each Power Four conference wins its conference and Boise State is the highest-ranked team from the other six conferences. No listed team has clinched a place by virtue of this projection.
